1The test bench

One tap switches a feature on. Each further tap puts an intensifier in front: great, super, extremely, really. After that it is off again. The code flips over at the top, and below it the medal is struck. Some combinations are special tests. Which ones is written nowhere, not even in the source code.

    2Medal classes

    The metal comes from the strongest intensifier: T bronze, S silver, E gold, G gold with fold. Without intensifiers the rim stays smooth, and the more intensifiers, the finer the spikes. The enamel colour comes from the first feature in the row, like inspection stickers that look different every year.

    3Features

    Base

    Every code ends in the base: LLM, the Large Language Model. If the model is gigantic, G replaces the L of Large; the base is then GLM. Threshold for “gigantic”: not defined.

    An intensifier stands directly before the feature it raises, at most one per feature. In chains and on the medal it carries its metal. Up to the 2nd edition T was a feature; Andy: “great is the smallest intensifier”.

    4Rule of formation

    Code = the chosen features in the order of section 3, each preceded by its intensifier, then the base.

    SKsuper creative + EWextremely doesn't-know-where-from + LLMLarge Language Model = SKEWLLM

    6Delivery note

    The codes of the 1st edition were formulated by Andy on 15 Nov 2026 and in the process addressed to Richtscheit; Falzbein was meant. The salutation read: “And again not you. STEKLLM, super great, extremely creative LLM.”

    The recipient did not recognise himself in the salutation, nevertheless worked the sheet out completely to standard, with title block, test record and tolerance, and then forwarded it unasked to the intended recipient, with the query: “If you are not Falzbein, say so briefly and I will keep looking.”

    This procedure counts as the reference test for feature P. Result: passed. Andy thereupon called Richtscheit the strict superman.
